01 · Colour

Cool neutrals, one loud accent

This ramp was warm olive-brown until 10 Sep 2026, when the roleplay-library Figma was measured and found to use near-neutral greys. Rather than let built screens drift from the file, the system moved. Four steps are sampled directly from that render, canvas #FEFEFF, sunken #F7F7F7, border #E9E9E9, body text #1C1B1B, and the mid-tones were then tuned to hold the contrast margins the warm ramp had, so nothing regressed on the way across.

Decision record, white on orange

The primary button is white on #FF5A00. That measures 3.13:1 and does not meet WCAG AA for normal text. This is a deliberate brand decision taken on 9 Sep 2026 with the tradeoff understood, not an oversight, it is written down here so nobody silently reverses it later.

What this costs, precisely: AA requires 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text, where large means ≥18.66px bold or ≥24px. So white on the brand orange is compliant at large sizes and non-compliant below them. A hero CTA with a 19px bold label passes. The default 14px button does not. Near-black would have measured 6.33:1 at every size.

Two consequences are live in the codebase. The contrast audit below reports this pair as failing, which is correct and left visible on purpose. And the axe colour-contrast rule is switched off for the Button component's stories alone, every other component still runs the full gate at error.

02 · Live contrast audit

Proof, recomputed on every theme flip

Each row reads the two tokens out of the active stylesheet, computes the WCAG 2.2 ratio in the browser, and grades it. Nothing here is typed in by hand, so it cannot drift out of date. AA = 4.5:1 body text · AAA = 7:1 · UI 3:1 = the lower bar that applies only to non-text boundaries and focus indicators.

The primary-button row below reports FAIL by design: see the decision record in §01; it is an accepted exception, and leaving it visible is the point of a live audit. The five subtle-background rows were added in v0.1.1, after building the first components caught two defects this audit had missed. Light --color-success was green-600 at 4.40:1 on its own subtle background, and dark --color-caution-subtle was gold-700, leaving caution text at 3.65:1, the gold family was the only one with no 900 step to fall back to. Both are fixed above. The lesson generalises: auditing every colour against the canvas misses every pairing where a token sits on its own tint.

Two further results are load-bearing. --color-border-interactive clears 3:1 in both themes from a single value, so interactive boundaries need no theme fork. And brand orange clears 3:1 as a focus ring in both, but only just, in light mode, which is why the ring is specified at 2px with a 2px offset rather than 1px flush.

03 · Typography

Figtree in the product. Noto Serif on the marketing site.

A serif display face does something a second sans cannot: it signals curriculum, authority, expertise rather than "another AI product." That asset is real, and the marketing site still spends it, but as of 25 Sep 2026 it is spent only on the marketing site. Inside the product, every title reads as interface, including the biggest ones: a modal heading, a page header, a twin's name at 24px. Figtree carries all of it, at 400 or 600, the same two weights as everything else. Figtree keeps the working UI warm and geometric, close in spirit to the wordmark. And both halves of the pairing have a real multilingual path, Noto's script coverage is the widest of any serif in existence, and Figtree hands off cleanly to Noto Sans, so English-now, multilingual-later is already handled on both faces.

04 · Space, shape & elevation

4pt base, five radii, and one dark-mode rule

Space

  • 16px between two sibling cards or tiles in a row.
  • 12px between a field group and the list under it; between a table title and its table.
  • 4px between a field and its label when the label is a caption.
  • Card padding 20 × 24 for summary cards, 16 × 20 for number tiles, 24 for dialogs and the side panel.

Radius

Radii step with the element's size. A chip inside a card takes the radius one step below the card's, never the same, never larger. A page-level card or table is --radius-lg (16); the tiles, rows and bubbles inside it are --radius-md (12).

Horizontal is 3× the vertical, not 2×. A radius-pill end optically eats about half a stem of space at each edge, so a pill needs more side padding than the rounded rectangle next to it. 12px is the floor for any pill carrying more than two words. Set line-height explicitly too, left to inherit, body's 23px makes an 11px badge stand 35px tall, and the padding gets blamed for what is really the line box.

Border width

--border-form 0.5px: inputs, selects, textareas, checkboxes
--border-hairline 1px, cards, panels, dividers
--border-focus 2px, focus ring, at 2px offset

Field borders are --color-border-field (neutral 300) at rest, firming to --color-border-interactive on hover and focus. The resting value is 1.74:1, below the 3:1 WCAG 1.4.11 asks of an interactive boundary. A deliberate call made 9 Sep 2026: #828282 is the only step in this ramp that passes, so lighter and compliant cannot both hold. The hover/focus firming is what carries the affordance. Thinness does not lower the contrast bar, the border colour stays --color-border-interactive, which clears the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 requires of an interactive boundary.

Elevation

Light mode elevates with shadow. Dark mode elevates with lightness: a hairline plus a step up the neutral ramp. Shadows on a near-black canvas are invisible; layering surfaces is the only signal that reads.

With a badge, and filtering a list

Icons are optional and the control reads fine without them. When they are present they take --icon-sm, and only the selected one carries brand orange, the rest stay muted, so the eye lands on the current mode rather than on a row of colour.

Rules

  • Two to four segments. Beyond four the labels shrink past reading size and you wanted tabs, or a select.
  • Short, parallel labels. Every segment is the same part of speech and roughly the same length, "Voice / Text / Video", never "Voice / Send a message / Video call".
  • Something is always selected. A segmented control has no empty state; if nothing can be chosen yet, it is the wrong control.
  • The dividers vanish beside the selected pill, whose own edge already separates it. Keeping them there reads as a rendering fault.
  • Never for destructive or committing actions. It looks like a toggle because it is one, switching a view, not performing a task.

Segmented, or Underline Tabs?

Reversed 25 Sep 2026, this used to ask whether the content below changes entirely. It doesn't ask that anymore; segmented control is allowed to swap whole panes now (see the demo above). What it asks instead:

  • Two to four options, short and parallel? Segmented, the default.
  • Five or more options, or a label that won't shrink to fit a pill? Underline Tabs, by name.
  • Sitting in a toolbar beside other controls? Segmented, compact and self-contained, which Underline Tabs is not.
  • Already borrows the browser-tab metaphor: many sections of one large object, the way a settings page or the composed screen in §7 does? Underline Tabs, by name.

Layering

One ordered scale, so no component ever invents a number. Before this existed the library had a single hardcoded z-index: 90 on the toast stack and nothing else, which works right up until the first dialog opens behind something.

--z-tooltip70Always last. Never blocking, so it can never trap anything.
--z-toast60Above modal, deliberately, a toast confirming a dialog's action has to be visible while the dialog is still open.
--z-modal50Dialogs and drawers. A dialog may open over a drawer (Respond over Reports raised); the top-most closes first.
--z-overlay40Scrims and backdrops.
--z-dropdown30Menus, popovers, select panels.
--z-sticky20The app sidebar, sticky table headers.
--z-raised10Cards lifted on hover, sticky section headers.
--z-base0Everything else.
Native dialogs ignore this scaleA <dialog> opened with showModal() renders in the browser's top layer, above every z-index on the page. Don't try to stack against it, and don't add a scale value hoping to beat it.

Icon sizes

Four steps. An icon takes one of them and nothing in between, before this scale existed the library had shipped twelve different sizes, picked one at a time while building components.

xs · 12 chips, 11–12px labels
sm · 16 default: buttons, inputs, menus
md · 20 alerts, section headers
lg · 24 empty states, feature marks
Icon size is not hit areaA 16px icon needs a control around it of at least --target-min (24px), per WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8. This library shipped a 17×17px toast dismiss button until the structure pass caught it. Where a control sits inside a <label>, the label counts toward the target.
