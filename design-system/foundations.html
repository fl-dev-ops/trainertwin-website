00 — Decision record

What's locked

These came out of the brief and are treated as fixed. Everything downstream derives from them, so changing one of these is a system-level change, not a tweak.

Locked

Primary is #FF5A00. Pure orange over the red-orange alternative, chosen so the entire red band stays free for error, destructive, and record states — non-negotiable in a product built on recording and deleting a trainer's material.

Locked

Decided 10 Sep 2026 after auditing it: of 700 colour literals across 273 unique values, 489 uses sat within a just-noticeable difference of an existing token — nobody chose #242424 over #2B2B2B. That is drift, and it is fixed by deriving: the landing keeps its own vocabulary (--l-night, --l-paper) but every value with a system equivalent points at it, so the two can no longer diverge. Rather than carve out an exception, the accent goes.

Locked

--color-live is red; --color-available is green. They are not the same idea. Recording is a warning — you are being captured — and borrows the red of a record light. Locked

Locked

01 — Colour

Cool neutrals, one loud accent

This ramp was warm olive-brown until 10 Sep 2026, when the roleplay-library Figma was measured and found to use near-neutral greys. Rather than let built screens drift from the file, the system moved. Four steps are sampled directly from that render — canvas #FEFEFF, sunken #F7F7F7, border #E9E9E9, body text #1C1B1B — and the mid-tones were then tuned to hold the contrast margins the warm ramp had, so nothing regressed on the way across.

Decision record — white on orange

The primary button is white on #FF5A00. That measures 3.13:1 and does not meet WCAG AA for normal text. This is a deliberate brand decision taken on 9 Sep 2026 with the tradeoff understood, not an oversight — it is written down here so nobody silently reverses it later.

What this costs, precisely: AA requires 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text, where large means ≥18.66px bold or ≥24px. So white on the brand orange is compliant at large sizes and non-compliant below them. A hero CTA with a 19px bold label passes. The default 14px button does not. Near-black would have measured 6.33:1 at every size.

Two consequences are live in the codebase. The contrast audit below reports this pair as failing, which is correct and left visible on purpose. And the axe colour-contrast rule is switched off for the Button component's stories alone — every other component still runs the full gate at error.

Primary action — correct vs. incorrect

Left, lg at 19px bold — 3.13:1 clears the 3:1 large-text bar, so this one is compliant. Middle, the 14px default: same colours, below the large-text threshold, so it is not. Use lg for hero CTAs.

02 — Live contrast audit

Proof, recomputed on every theme flip

Each row reads the two tokens out of the active stylesheet, computes the WCAG 2.2 ratio in the browser, and grades it. Nothing here is typed in by hand, so it cannot drift out of date. AA = 4.5:1 body text · AAA = 7:1 · UI 3:1 = the lower bar that applies only to non-text boundaries and focus indicators.

The primary-button row below reports FAIL by design — see the decision record in §01; it is an accepted exception, and leaving it visible is the point of a live audit. The five subtle-background rows were added in v0.1.1, after building the first components caught two defects this audit had missed. Light --color-success was green-600 at 4.40:1 on its own subtle background, and dark --color-caution-subtle was gold-700, leaving caution text at 3.65:1 — the gold family was the only one with no 900 step to fall back to. Both are fixed above. The lesson generalises: auditing every colour against the canvas misses every pairing where a token sits on its own tint.

Two further results are load-bearing. --color-border-interactive clears 3:1 in both themes from a single value, so interactive boundaries need no theme fork. And brand orange clears 3:1 as a focus ring in both — but only just, in light mode, which is why the ring is specified at 2px with a 2px offset rather than 1px flush.

03 — Typography

Noto Serif over Figtree

A serif display face does something a second sans cannot: it signals curriculum, authority, expertise rather than "another AI product." That is precisely the asset TrainerTwin is selling. Rules

  • Two weights, 400 and 600 — nothing else. 400 for everything read, 600 for everything titled, labelled, or clickable. 700 is out of the scale: at 13–15px it closes up Figtree's counters without reading as more important than 600. The scale lives in --tt-weight-regular / --tt-weight-semibold; a hard-coded weight anywhere else is a bug.
  • Serif is for titles only. Noto Serif above 20px, never for body, labels, or UI controls. It is the voice of authorship, not of interface.
  • Figtree owns everything operable. Buttons, inputs, nav, tables, transcript.
  • Mono owns anything counted. Scores, durations, timestamps, token names, source counts — always with tabular-nums.
  • Running text stops at 65ch. Applies to transcripts and twin answers, which are the longest text in the product.

Script-safe now, multilingual later

  • No fixed heights on text containers. Devanagari and Tamil ascenders/descenders exceed Latin metrics; fixed rows clip.
  • Line-height in unitless ratios, never px, so it scales with the substituted face.
  • No text baked into images — including marketing hero art and the twin's cover graphics.
  • No fixed-width labels or buttons. Budget ~35% expansion.
  • Fallback chain declared today: Noto Serif → Noto Serif Devanagari; Figtree → Noto Sans → Noto Sans Devanagari.
04 — Space, shape & elevation

4pt base, five radii, and one dark-mode rule

Space

Radius

Radii step with the element's size. A chip inside a card takes the radius one step below the card's — never the same, never larger.

Pill padding

12 Twin configuration Scaling teams
--pad-pill-sm 2px 8px — counts, status dots, inline flags
--pad-pill-md 4px 12px — badges, tags, eyebrow labels (default)
--pad-pill-lg 6px 16px — filter chips, stat pills, anything clickable

Horizontal is 3× the vertical, not 2×. A radius-pill end optically eats about half a stem of space at each edge, so a pill needs more side padding than the rounded rectangle next to it. 12px is the floor for any pill carrying more than two words. Set line-height explicitly too — left to inherit, body's 23px makes an 11px badge stand 35px tall, and the padding gets blamed for what is really the line box.

Border width

--border-form 0.5px — inputs, selects, textareas, checkboxes
--border-hairline 1px — cards, panels, dividers
--border-focus 2px — focus ring, at 2px offset

Field borders are --color-border-field (neutral 300) at rest, firming to --color-border-interactive on hover and focus. The resting value is 1.74:1 — below the 3:1 WCAG 1.4.11 asks of an interactive boundary. A deliberate call made 9 Sep 2026: #828282 is the only step in this ramp that passes, so lighter and compliant cannot both hold. The hover/focus firming is what carries the affordance. Forms take the hairline. On a 2× display 0.5px is a true one-device-pixel rule; on 1×, browsers round up to 1px rather than dropping it, so the boundary never vanishes. Thinness does not lower the contrast bar — the border colour stays --color-border-interactive, which clears the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 requires of an interactive boundary.

Elevation

Light mode elevates with shadow. Dark mode elevates with lightness — a hairline plus a step up the neutral ramp. Shadows on a near-black canvas are invisible; layering surfaces is the only signal that reads.

05 — Motion · provisional

Where all the energy lives

You deferred the expressiveness call, so this is scoped to purposeful: motion only where it carries meaning. Worth remembering why the stakes are unusual here — because the brand spends nothing on decorative colour or density, felt liveliness in the learner session is carried entirely by these five cases.

Tokens

The five cases that must animate

  • Thinking. The gap between a learner finishing and the twin starting. Never a spinner — spinners read as "broken," and this gap is the single most trust-critical moment in the product.
  • Streaming. Text and transcript arriving progressively. Perceived latency, not real latency, is what makes a twin feel present.
  • Turn handoff. Whose turn it is, in all three modes. In voice this replaces the visual cues a real interviewer would give.
  • Score reveal. The payoff moment of a mock interview. Staged, not instant — the only place a short --duration-deliberate is licensed.
  • Ingest progress. Uploading and processing a trainer's material is slow and anxious. Show real progress per source, never an indeterminate bar.

07 — Signature patterns

The four things only TrainerTwin needs

Everything above this line is craft any good product needs. These four are the ones that come out of your specific brief — always-disclosed, always-cited, three modes in one frame — and they're where the design system stops being generic.

1 · The AI disclosure marker

Persistent, quiet, and never orange — orange means the platform is acting, and the disclosure is a statement of fact, not a call to action. Neutral-on-neutral lands at 7.9:1 in both themes, which is legible without ever competing with the session.

Session header, all three modes
PRAI twin of Priya Raman · not Priya
  • Never dismissible, and never below the fold of the session frame.
  • In video mode it appears twice — once in the header, and once as a persistent badge composited inside the video frame itself. A likeness of a real person that can be screen-recorded must carry its disclosure inside the pixels, not beside them.
  • Announced at session start to assistive tech, before the first twin turn.
  • Names the trainer, because the trust being extended is theirs. "AI assistant" is not a disclosure; "AI twin of Priya Raman" is.

2 · Citation & source confidence

You chose always-cited. The design consequence is that answers need three states, not two — and the third is the one that protects a trainer's reputation when the twin is asked something they never taught.

Source chips — beneath the twin's answer

  • Grounded — traced to a specific location in an uploaded source. Green dot, source name always visible, never hidden behind a hover.
  • Inferred — extrapolated from the trainer's demonstrated style or pedagogy rather than a specific passage. Blue dot. Honest about being a reconstruction.
  • Outside scope — general knowledge the trainer never covered. The only one that gets caution colour and a visible border, because it's the only one where the trainer's name is attached to something that isn't theirs.
  • Chips sit below the answer, not inline. Inline superscripts interrupt reading; a mock interview under time pressure cannot afford that.

A mock interview scorecard is the most differentiated surface you have and the one most likely to be screenshotted and shared. It gets a deliberately restricted palette.

08 — Component library

Every element, in both themes

Live specimens, not screenshots — each one is built from the semantic token layer, so flipping the theme in the sidebar re-renders all of it. Forms use the 0.5px hairline; the primary button uses the white-on-orange decision recorded in §01.

Buttons

Five variants, in descending emphasis: primary (the directed moment) → strong (the everyday action) → secondary → ghost, with danger outside the ladder. Destructive is outlined, not filled — a filled destructive button carries the same visual weight as the primary action, which is how people delete a trainer's uploaded material by mistake.

Variants
Sizes — sm 32 · md 40 · lg 50

Only lg meets AA. Its 19px bold label clears the 3:1 large-text bar for white on orange; sm and md sit below the threshold. Use lg for hero and primary-path CTAs.

States
Icon with text · icon alone · notification count

An icon-only button always carries an aria-label. Without one a screen reader announces "button" and nothing else, which is the most common accessibility failure in a toolbar. The icon itself is aria-hidden — it is decoration once the label exists.

Secondary at each size

Icon-only buttons are square at every size — 32 / 40 / 50 — so a toolbar of them keeps a rhythm instead of going oblong.

The count badge is the smallest instance of the white-on-orange exceptionAt 10.5px, white on #FF5A00 is well below the large-text threshold that makes the lg button compliant. It is legible because a one- or two-digit number is a shape you recognise rather than text you read — but if this ever carries anything longer, switch its label to near-black, which measures 6.32:1.

Which CTA, where

Brand orange is spent on the single most important action in view — not on every action that happens to be important. In a list where five rows each carry a button, five orange buttons mean none of them reads as the one to press.

Dropdown

A custom listbox rather than a native <select>, because native options cannot carry a description, a group label, or a disabled reason. Be aware that the two look nothing alike when open. A native select's popup is drawn by the operating system, not by the page — you can style the closed control, but the open menu ignores every token here, and on macOS it renders as a grey system panel. That is a browser constraint, not a gap in the system. So the choice between them is partly a visual one: if the dropdown opens somewhere the brand needs to hold, it has to be this listbox. Use the native one when the choice is short and self-evident — it is more robust on mobile and costs nothing.

Segmented control — same session, different mode

With a badge, and filtering a list

Icons are optional and the control reads fine without them. When they are present they take --icon-sm, and only the selected one carries brand orange — the rest stay muted, so the eye lands on the current mode rather than on a row of colour.

Rules

  • Two to four segments. Beyond four the labels shrink past reading size and you wanted tabs, or a select.
  • Short, parallel labels. Every segment is the same part of speech and roughly the same length — "Voice / Text / Video", never "Voice / Send a message / Video call".
  • Something is always selected. A segmented control has no empty state; if nothing can be chosen yet, it is the wrong control.
  • The dividers vanish beside the selected pill, whose own edge already separates it. Keeping them there reads as a rendering fault.
  • Never for destructive or committing actions. It looks like a toggle because it is one — switching a view, not performing a task.

Tabs or segmented?

  • Does the content below change entirely? Tabs.
  • Does the same content change form? Segmented control.
  • More than four options, or long labels? Tabs.
  • Does it belong in a toolbar beside other controls? Segmented — it is compact and self-contained, which tabs are not.

  • Night is a scope, not a theme. .c-night re-points tokens inside one section. Never reach for [data-theme="dark"] to build a dark band — that flips the whole page for the viewer.
  • Availability green is not success green, and not --color-live. --color-live is red because recording is a warning; --color-available is green because it is reassurance. Opposite messages, never interchangeable.

Container widths

--container-prose65chRunning text. The reading-measure cap.

--container-md900pxArticles, settings, detail panes.

A container caps how wide content grows. A breakpoint decides when the layout changes shape. They are different jobs and the values are not interchangeable.

Breakpoints

sm640pxLarge phone, landscape.
md768pxTablet. Sidebar returns.
lg1024pxLaptop. The design target.
xl1280pxDesktop.
2xl1536pxLarge desktop.

These match Tailwind's defaults on purpose. There is no reason to invent our own, and diverging would surprise anyone who has used Tailwind before. Note that CSS cannot read a custom property inside a media query — the values live in @theme in globals.css, which is what generates the sm: / md: variants.

Layering

One ordered scale, so no component ever invents a number. Before this existed the library had a single hardcoded z-index: 90 on the toast stack and nothing else — which works right up until the first dialog opens behind something.

--z-tooltip70Always last. Never blocking, so it can never trap anything.
--z-toast60Above modal, deliberately — a toast confirming a dialog's action has to be visible while the dialog is still open.
--z-modal50Dialogs and drawers.
--z-overlay40Scrims and backdrops.
--z-dropdown30Menus, popovers, select panels.
--z-sticky20The app sidebar, sticky table headers.
--z-raised10Cards lifted on hover, sticky section headers.
--z-base0Everything else.
Native dialogs ignore this scaleA <dialog> opened with showModal() renders in the browser's top layer, above every z-index on the page. Don't try to stack against it — and don't add a scale value hoping to beat it.

Icon sizes

Four steps. An icon takes one of them and nothing in between — before this scale existed the library had shipped twelve different sizes, picked one at a time while building components.

xs · 12 chips, 11–12px labels
sm · 16 default — buttons, inputs, menus
md · 20 alerts, section headers
lg · 24 empty states, feature marks
Icon size is not hit areaA 16px icon needs a control around it of at least --target-min (24px), per WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8. This library shipped a 17×17px toast dismiss button until the structure pass caught it. Where a control sits inside a <label>, the label counts toward the target.
