TrainerTwin · Illustration · Direction study

Grid, Paper, Echo

Three ways TrainerTwin could draw. Each one is rendered in your real tokens and applied to the same two surfaces — a marketing hero and the Studio's empty state — so you're judging them in place rather than as floating art.

Pick one and it becomes the asset. The style spec and a handful of reference pieces are what make an illustration system consistent; the generator is a weekend of work either way. All three below are flat vector, so Claude can draw them straight into your tokens — no image model, no per-image cost, and they recolour for dark mode for free. Flip your theme and watch every illustration on this page follow.
Direction 1

Grid

Extend the logo instead of inventing beside it. Every illustration is built from the same rounded cell the mark is built from — a 3×3 module, r=20 corners, right angles only. The system draws itself.

Vocabulary
Marketing heroMany unlike sources become one structured twin, which serves many alike learners. The argument is in the alignment, not in a metaphor.
Studio · no sources yetOne cell filled, eight waiting. Progress is legible without a progress bar.

Rules that become the spec

  • Right angles only. No diagonals, no curves except corner radii and rail elbows.
  • Corner radius is 13.6% of the cell, matching the mark's r=20 on a 147 cell. Every shape, every size.
  • Three fill weights: solid primary, tinted, dashed outline. Muted neutrals for anything not yet the product's own.
  • Orange is reserved for the twin and for what the twin produces. Sources are always neutral.

Where it breaks

  • It cannot draw a person. Anything about nerves before an interview, or a trainer's warmth, is out of reach.
  • It reads cool. Precise and systematic — which is half your brand, but only half.
  • It gets samey at volume. Past a dozen pieces the constraint starts showing as repetition rather than rigour.
Direction 2

Paper

Draw the material, not the machine. Sheets, margin notes, highlighter, tabs — the physical residue of teaching. It shows what the twin is actually made of, and it is the warmest of the three by a distance.

Vocabulary
Marketing heroThe highlighter stroke leaves the page and becomes the twin's voice — one continuous line from a trainer's own marked-up material to an answer.
Studio · no sources yetAn unmarked page with the highlighter already waiting. The invitation is in the empty swipe.

Rules that become the spec

  • Every scene contains one sheet and at least one human mark on it — a tick, a highlight, a circle.
  • Orange is only ever a human mark. Highlighter, annotation, and the voice that comes out. Never the paper.
  • Text is bars, never lettering. Keeps it language-agnostic, which matters when you localise.
  • One deliberate imperfection per piece — a 1–2° rotation on the highlighter. That tilt is the whole warmth budget.

Where it breaks

  • It is the most crowded neighbourhood in edtech. Sheets-and-highlighters is the default; only art direction keeps it off the shelf.
  • It skews backward-looking. Paper is what trainers had before you; nothing here says AI.
  • The session side is hard. Live voice and mock interviews have no paper in them.
Direction 3

Echo

Draw the twin literally. Every subject appears twice — a solid form and an offset outline of itself, in orange. The product's name becomes the drawing rule, so the concept is legible before anyone reads a word.

Vocabulary
Marketing heroThe trainer is solid and singular; the twin is the orange outline that keeps going after they stop. The arcs are reach they could not have on their own.
Studio · no sources yetAn echo with nothing to echo. The absence is the message, and it needs no copy to land.

Rules that become the spec

  • Two of everything. A solid neutral form and its orange outline, offset up and to the right by ~20% of the form's width.
  • The echo is always orange and always outline. It never fills. That is what makes it read as a copy rather than a second person.
  • Arcs radiate from the echo, never the solid. The reach belongs to the twin.
  • Figures stay abstract — circle and shoulder, no faces, no skin tone, no gender cues.

Where it breaks

  • The device is loud and finite. After twenty pieces the offset outline is a tic, and there is no second idea behind it.
  • Faceless figures are their own cliché, adjacent to the corporate-blob look you would be sold by any stock library.
  • It can imply the twin replaces the trainer — precisely the reading your AI-disclosure work exists to prevent.

What I'd do

Grid as the base system, borrowing Paper's marks for the Studio. Grid is the only one of the three that is unarguably yours, costs almost nothing to keep consistent, and never fights the interface. Its real weakness is that it can't carry warmth — so where warmth actually matters, which is the trainer's own workspace and the moment before a mock interview, let the human marks in: a highlighter swipe, a margin tick. Echo I'd keep as a single recurring motif for the twin itself, not a whole system; it's too loud to repeat and it risks saying the twin replaces the trainer.

Whichever you pick, the next step is the same: I write ILLUSTRATION-SPEC.md next to your design system file, draw four canonical pieces as reference, and add an <Illustration> component so sections request art by name. That is layers 1 and 2 — the part that actually determines quality. The generator after that is trivial, and still needs no image model.
